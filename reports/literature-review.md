# Literature Review: Spatial Context vs. Spectral Signatures for Solar PV Detection (with focus on Japan)

Scope: methods for detecting/mapping solar photovoltaic (PV) installations in satellite/aerial imagery, organized around the paper's central axis — spatial-context deep learning (CNN semantic segmentation / object detection) vs. spectral-signature methods (band indices, pixel-wise classifiers such as Random Forest/XGBoost) — with emphasis on Japan-specific and benchmark-design literature. 16 sources: 6 supplied by the user, 6 added via web search, 3 follow-up sources the user retrieved to close gaps flagged in the original pass, and 1 (Kruitwagen et al.) retrieved after being identified as missing from the exposé's own bibliography.

## 2. Consensus

- **CNN semantic segmentation generally outperforms pixel-wise spectral/RF classification on accuracy (IoU/F-score) when adequate labeled data exists** — clearest in the Brazil benchmark (#2) and the hybrid global dataset (#11), consistent with DeepSolar's (#14) precision/recall in the 88–94% range and Ishii et al.'s (#13) result that their from-scratch CNN "significantly outperformed the state of the art" for Japan in 2016.
- **Training-label quality matters at least as much as architecture choice.** The Brazil benchmark (#2) found four architectures × four backbones converge in performance once labels are high-quality — a methodological point directly relevant to your benchmark design and error analysis.
- **Spectral-index / traditional-ML methods remain the default for national-scale operational mapping** in the papers actually covering large territories (#1, #4, #5) — chosen for compute cost and lower label requirements rather than for superior accuracy. The SPPI paper (#15) makes this an explicit design goal rather than an incidental tradeoff.
- **PV siting is not independent of land-use class** — installations concentrate on cropland, barren land, forest edges, and (in Japan specifically) abandoned farmland (#1, #5, #6). This is the empirical basis for why *spatial context* (neighborhood land-use signal) should carry information beyond a pixel's raw spectral reflectance — the theoretical crux of your title.
- **Multi-spectral channel choice materially affects CNN performance**, independent of the spatial-vs-spectral framing: Ishii et al. (#13) show combining all 7 Landsat-8 bands (not just RGB) is critical to their CNN's accuracy — evidence that "spatial" and "spectral" are not cleanly separable axes in practice, which your benchmark's discussion section should address directly rather than treat the two as fully orthogonal.

## 3. Disagreements / tensions

- **Efficiency vs. accuracy framing.** SPPI (#15) argues spectral indices are ~1,240× faster than deep learning and sufficient for operational mapping, achieving up to 96% OA in its best-case environment. This sits in direct tension with the segmentation literature (#2, #11, #14) showing materially higher IoU/F-score for CNNs on heterogeneous or small-scale installations, and with Ishii et al. (#13) explicitly beating prior (non-CNN) state of the art on Japanese data. **This tension is the central axis your benchmark paper should adjudicate empirically for the Japan case**, rather than assume either side. Note SPPI's 96% figure is its *best-case* environment (e.g., desert) — its accuracy is not uniform across the five environment types it tests, which is itself evidence that spectral-only methods may be more context-sensitive than their headline number suggests.
- **No consistent "winning" architecture across regions/resolutions.** Brazil (#2) favors U-Net+EfficientNet-b7; the global hybrid study (#11) uses U-Net differently (as a seed-labeler, not the final classifier); DeepSolar (#14) uses a comparatively simple CNN classifier converted for localization; the non-peer-reviewed object-detection comparison (#9) tests YOLO variants against Mask R-CNN without a clear universal winner. Architecture performance appears resolution- and dataset-dependent — an open question your Japan-specific benchmark can speak to directly.

## 4. Gaps this paper addresses

1. **No existing study runs a controlled spatial-CNN-vs-spectral-signature benchmark on Japan PV data using modern architectures.** The only national Japan PV map (#5) is explicitly XGBoost + spectral index, not CNN. The two Japan CNN papers (#13, #7) predate modern U-Net/DeepLab-class segmentation — #13 (2016) is a from-scratch classification-to-FCN network, #7 (2017) is a weakly-supervised feedback-CNN — and neither is framed as a spatial-vs-spectral comparison against a contemporary index-based baseline like SPPI (#15) or XGBoost (#5).
2. **Japan-specific land-use drivers are documented but not yet connected to a CNN model's spatial error patterns.** #5 and #6 establish *where* PV concentrates and *why* (forest/farmland conversion, abandonment drivers) but neither uses this to diagnose a detector's false positives/negatives — a natural discussion-section link for your paper (e.g., does a spatial-context CNN confuse PV with greenhouse plastic on abandoned farmland the way a spectral index would?).
3. **The Japan research trajectory (Ishii #13 → Imamoglu et al. #7 → Shimada & Takeuchi #5) moves from CNN toward XGBoost+index for the national-scale case** — plausibly a compute/interpretability tradeoff rather than an accuracy verdict, since #13 already demonstrated CNNs beating prior state of the art on Japanese imagery back in 2016. Nobody has re-opened the CNN question for Japan with a direct, apples-to-apples comparison against a modern spectral-index method. That is precisely the gap your title stakes out.
4. **The spectral-channel-contribution question Ishii et al. (#13) raised in 2016 (does using all available bands, not just RGB, matter for CNN accuracy?) has not been revisited with modern segmentation architectures or against a spectral-index baseline as strong as SPPI (#15).** This is a concrete, citable sub-question your benchmark's ablations could resolve.
